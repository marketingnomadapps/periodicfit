## How BMI is calculated

Body mass index compares your weight with your height:

**BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²**

In imperial units, it's weight (lb) × 703 ÷ height (in)². This calculator handles both, so just pick your units.

## WHO adult BMI categories

- **Under 18.5:** underweight
- **18.5–24.9:** healthy weight
- **25–29.9:** overweight
- **30 and above:** obese range

The marker on the colour scale shows where you sit. The healthy weight range shows the weights that give a BMI of 18.5–24.9 at your height.

## What BMI is good for

BMI is quick, free and useful for spotting trends across large groups. For an individual, it's a reasonable first screening tool. It's linked with risks such as high blood pressure and type 2 diabetes at the population level.

## What BMI misses

- **Muscle vs fat.** A strong lifter and an inactive person can have the same BMI with very different health.
- **Where fat is stored.** Fat around the waist carries more health risk than fat elsewhere. A waist measurement adds useful information.
- **Ethnicity.** Many South Asian adults face higher metabolic risk at lower BMIs, so some guidelines use lower cut-offs.
- **Age and sex.** Body composition naturally changes with age.
