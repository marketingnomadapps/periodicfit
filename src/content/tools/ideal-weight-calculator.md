## How this calculator works

You get two kinds of results.

**1. Healthy weight range.** The weights that give a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 at your height. This is the range the World Health Organization uses for healthy adults.

**2. Classic ideal body weight formulas.** Each one starts from a base weight at 5 feet (152.4 cm) and adds a fixed amount for every inch above that:

| Formula | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| Robinson (1983) | 52 kg + 1.9 kg/inch | 49 kg + 1.7 kg/inch |
| Miller (1983) | 56.2 kg + 1.41 kg/inch | 53.1 kg + 1.36 kg/inch |
| Devine (1974) | 50 kg + 2.3 kg/inch | 45.5 kg + 2.3 kg/inch |
| Hamwi (1964) | 48 kg + 2.7 kg/inch | 45.5 kg + 2.2 kg/inch |

## How to read your result

Think of the formulas as reference points inside your healthy range rather than one magic number. If all four cluster around a similar weight, that's a reasonable target for someone with average muscle mass.

Your body type matters. People with a larger frame or more muscle often feel and perform best toward the top of the range. Smaller-framed people often sit lower.

## Setting a goal you can keep

1. **Pick a range, not a number.** A 3–4 kg band is easier to maintain than a single target.
2. **Look at body fat, not just weight.** Check yours with the [body fat calculator](/tools/body-fat-calculator).
3. **Plan the time it takes.** The [transformation timeline](/tools/transformation-timeline) turns your goal into a realistic date.
4. **Focus on habits.** Daily steps, protein, sleep and strength training shape your weight more than any formula.

## Limitations

Ideal weight formulas don't account for muscle, bone structure, age or ethnicity. They're a starting point for adults, not medical advice. If you're unsure what's healthy for you, ask your doctor or one of our coaches.
