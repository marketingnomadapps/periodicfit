## How the macro calculator works

1. **Calories.** We estimate your maintenance calories with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation and your activity level, then adjust for your goal: about 20% less for fat loss, maintenance as is, or 10% more for muscle gain.
2. **Protein first.** Protein is set per kilogram of body weight: 2.0 g/kg for fat loss, 1.6 g/kg for maintenance and 1.8 g/kg for muscle gain. These sit inside the 1.6–2.2 g/kg range supported by sports nutrition research.
3. **Fats next.** Fats are set as a share of calories (30% balanced, 40% lower-carb, 20% higher-carb), with a floor of 0.6 g/kg.
4. **Carbs fill the rest.** Whatever calories remain become carbohydrates, your main fuel for training.

## Choosing a diet style

- **Balanced** suits most people and most cuisines, including typical Indian meals.
- **Lower carb** can help if you prefer eggs, paneer, nuts and fattier foods and feel better with fewer starches.
- **Higher carb** is for athletes and people training hard most days, who need more fuel.

All three styles work for fat loss and muscle gain when calories and protein are on point.

## Hitting your macros with real food

- **Protein:** dal, chana, rajma, paneer, curd, Greek yogurt, eggs, chicken, fish, tofu, soy chunks, whey.
- **Carbs:** rice, roti, oats, poha, potatoes, fruit, millets.
- **Fats:** nuts, seeds, ghee, oils, cheese, avocado.

Spread protein across 3–5 meals. The per-meal line under your result shows a simple target for each.

## Limitations

These targets are for healthy adults. People with kidney disease, diabetes or other medical conditions should get personalised advice from a doctor or dietitian before changing their protein or carb intake.
