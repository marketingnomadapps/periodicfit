## How the split builder works

Every training plan answers three questions: **how often**, **what to do**, and **how hard**. The builder handles all three.

**How often.** Your days per week decide the structure:

- **2 days:** Full Body A and B, so every muscle is trained twice a week.
- **3 days:** three full-body sessions, the classic plan for beginners and busy people.
- **4 days:** Upper / Lower, twice each. A favourite for steady muscle and strength gains.
- **5 days:** Push, Pull, Legs, then Upper and Lower.
- **6 days:** Push, Pull, Legs, twice. Best for experienced lifters who recover well.

**What to do.** Each day is built from movement patterns, not random exercises: squat, hinge, horizontal and vertical push, horizontal and vertical pull, lunge, glutes, core and arms. Exercises are then picked for your equipment, and variations rotate so day A and day B aren't identical.

**How hard.** Sets and reps change with your goal:

- **Build muscle:** 3–4 sets of 6–15 reps, with more volume on the main lifts.
- **Get stronger:** a heavy main lift for 5 sets of 3–5, then accessories for 6–8.
- **Lose fat:** 3 sets of 10–15, plus a short conditioning finisher.
- **General fitness:** 3 sets of 8–12 and one conditioning finisher a week.

## Making the plan work

1. **Warm up for 5–10 minutes.** Light cardio, then two or three lighter sets of your first exercise.
2. **Leave 1–3 reps in the tank** on most sets. Training to failure every set is not needed and slows recovery.
3. **Log your weights.** Progress you can see keeps you motivated. Use the [one-rep max calculator](/tools/one-rep-max-calculator) to pick working weights.
4. **Run the plan for 6–8 weeks** before changing exercises. Consistency beats novelty.
5. **Sleep 7–9 hours.** Training is the stimulus, but sleep is where you recover.
